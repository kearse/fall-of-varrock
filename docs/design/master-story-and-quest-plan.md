FALL OF VARROCK
Master Story & Quest Plan
Consolidated Narrative Authority • September 2026
This document consolidates the story, regional campaigns, revelation structure, quest architecture, and narrative decisions already planned. It intentionally marks the post–first-assault ending as unfinished rather than inventing an ending that has not yet been designed.

## 1. Narrative Design Rules

Open world first. Story gives direction and meaning; it does not grant permission to play the game.
Main Story = the Varrock spine. Regional Campaigns = the body of the story. Open world = how the player lives between and through those arcs.
Regions may be strategically mandatory without making every quest, boss, activity, or playstyle inside them mandatory.
Use existing OSRS/FoV systems instead of bespoke quest mechanics wherever they can deliver the same story beat.
Custom quest development is reserved for memorable moments, revelations, contained battles, and unique narrative payoffs.
Persistent multiplayer comes first: avoid quest states that require incompatible large overworld transformations.
FoV never assumes the player completed or remembers old OSRS quests. Past OSRS events are history learned from people who lived them.
Classic OSRS bosses remain mechanically faithful. FoV-original encounters are where custom boss design belongs.
Current story/world decisions are the narrative authority. Older repository concepts are legacy unless reaffirmed here.

## 3. What Actually Caused The Fall

The public history is wrong or incomplete. The true history is uncovered gradually.

| STATUS | STORY ELEMENT | CURRENT PLAN |
| --- | --- | --- |
| WORKING CANON | Zemouregal | Launches the largest undead assault against Varrock and forces Arrav to lead it. Humans are beginning to resist successfully before the wider convergence. |
| WORKING CANON | Arrav | Publicly becomes the face of Varrock's destruction, but secretly resists Zemouregal, slows the assault, helps civilians escape, and disappears during the catastrophe. |
| WORKING CANON | Sliske | The mastermind behind the convergence. Attempts to amplify the Elder Horn through buried Senntisten infrastructure to gain control over Mahjarrat and break the Ritual cycle. |
| WORKING CANON | Lucien | Possesses the Stone of Jas. Detects the threat and ultimately overloads the Senntisten network rather than submit to Sliske's control. |
| WORKING CANON | Dragonkin | Converge because of the Stone of Jas and add another catastrophic force to the battlefield. |
| WORKING CANON | Senntisten Network | FoV-original buried Zarosian conduits beneath/east of Varrock that amplify the collision of Elder Artefacts, ancient magic, and necromancy. |
| OPEN | The thing beneath Senntisten | The collapse awakens something none of Sliske, Lucien, Zemouregal, or the others understood. Exact identity/nature requires a dedicated later lore pass. |

Sliske intended control, not the destruction of Varrock. The Fall is the catastrophic failure of his plan.

## 5. Player Identity and the Missing Adventurer

The player is secretly the child of the original legendary Adventurer. This is not a royal bloodline and does not make the player inherently entitled to rule.
The player's guardian claimed the father died during the Fall and deliberately hid the true identity.
The Adventurer's physical appearance is never rigidly canonized.
Older characters can recognize mannerisms, resemblance, or familiar behavior without immediately revealing the truth.
The coalition chooses to trust the player before the lineage reveal. Authority is earned through rebuilding and service.
The father's ultimate fate remains open.

### Breadcrumb Plan

An older guard notices a familiar mannerism.
The Wise Old Man asks an unusually pointed question about the player's father.
A surviving guard from the Fall recognizes something familiar.
Temple Knight records contain redactions or references the player cannot initially interpret.
A Senntisten echo preserves part of the Adventurer's final words: “If I don't return…”
My Arm initially mistakes the player for the Adventurer, then realizes the player is younger but resembles him.
Vanescula may show subtle suspicion without explaining why.
The guardian eventually confirms the truth late in the campaign.

The early main campaign gets the player into the war quickly, then opens the world rather than keeping the player in a linear quest hallway.

## 8. Opening Campaign Detail

### The Last Free City

Player spawns as a Peasant during an attack/probe against Lumbridge rather than during a peaceful tutorial.
The player's first meaningful act is standing and fighting when escape would be understandable.
Surviving defenders notice the player.
Basic combat objectives replace abstract recruit trials.
A supply objective replaces equipment consumed/lost during the defense and naturally demonstrates gathering/production.
The opening should introduce the idea that everyone—fighters and skillers—keeps the kingdom alive.
Do not use the old recurring General Zo defensive siege system as the permanent gameplay loop; this is a story opening, not the core war architecture.

### Early War Exposure

The player should see or join a March in the first session. FoV's signature living-war system should not be hidden behind hours of conventional questing.

## 11. Wilderness — UNDERSTAND I

| STATUS | STORY ELEMENT | CURRENT PLAN |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PROPOSED | The First Scar | The player learns that the Wilderness is not simply a dangerous wasteland; it is evidence of an earlier catastrophe involving the Stone of Jas. |
| PROPOSED | The Stone's Shadow | Connect the Stone's history to Lucien and establish that Lucien possessed it during the Fall. |

The Wilderness arc does not prove Lucien caused the Fall by himself. Its purpose is to break the simplistic Zemouregal/Arrav explanation and send the investigation toward the Desert/Mahjarrat.
Required story objectives must not require killing human players. PvP risk may exist as part of travel/open-world danger, but human PKing is never a mandatory quest objective.

By the end of the Desert arc, the player understands that Lucien + Stone and Sliske + Horn converged at Senntisten. The exact physical mechanism of the catastrophe remains unresolved until later.

## 13. Kandarin — SUSTAIN

Working major quest title: The Long Road East.
King Thoros is alive and rules.
Kandarin is not a generic destroyed kingdom; it remains comparatively prosperous but disconnected from the eastern war.
Primary goal is restoration of trade/logistics between surviving powers.
Spirit Trees, gnome gliders, and Fairy Rings can be restored/improved where the Fall plausibly disrupted connections.
Gnomes are not simply another army/supply faction; their distinctive payoff is transportation and connectivity.
First Eastern Convoy should be a contained/instanced or repeatable system rather than shared-world carts permanently moving across the map.
War Effort / Realm Supplies can provide the gameplay threshold that turns restored trade into actual SUSTAIN capability.
Regional payoff: eastern trade, improved transport, convoy activity, and logistics satisfy or materially advance SUSTAIN.

## 14. Morytania — SECURE

Preserve the post–Blood Moon Rises world: Lowerniel Drakan is defeated; do not resurrect him or replay the old Myreque war.

| STATUS | STORY ELEMENT | CURRENT PLAN |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PROPOSED | Across the Salve | The player enters a tense post-Drakan political environment and discovers actions designed to provoke renewed war across the Salve. |
| PROPOSED | The Salve Accord | Expose/prevent the false-flag escalation and create an enforceable border agreement. |

Vanescula survives as a morally complicated political figure rather than a replacement Drakan.
The hostile/expansionist faction should not simply be 'Drakan 2.0.'
The accord is not a cheerful alliance: no invasion/crossing, mutual action against violators, and enough stability for Misthalin to commit forces north.
Temple Trekking can be reused/recontextualized for a special escort or travel sequence instead of building a bespoke system.
Regional payoff: SECURE.

## 15. Council of Gielinor

Occurs after the strategic prerequisites are solved.
Small representative meeting in an existing or dedicated interior—roughly 6–8 meaningful representatives rather than armies filling Lumbridge.
Reviews BREACH, SECURE, UNDERSTAND, and SUSTAIN.
Tiffy can provide urgency based on what the investigation now suggests about Varrock/Senntisten.
The player is not suddenly named Supreme General.
The coalition has already watched the player solve problems across Gielinor; trust is earned before the lineage reveal.
The council authorizes sustained assaults on Fallen Varrock, not permanent reclamation.

## 16. First Major Varrock Assault

This is the payoff to the regional phase and the transition into the unfinished late main story.
Use the existing public war framework rather than a giant bespoke single-player battle.
The player does not need to personally start the war; participation is open even when command authority is rank-gated.
The assault should reach meaningfully deeper into Varrock than ordinary Marches.
Victory is temporary battlefield success, not ownership.
The event should reveal new information, access, or evidence that forces the story beneath the accepted history of the Fall.
Meaningful participation can award Veteran of Varrock, which later contributes to higher-rank eligibility.
Varrock remains hostile after the story event and becomes a repeatable endgame pillar.

### What This Assault Must NOT Do

Do not permanently liberate districts.
Do not turn Varrock into a safe city.
Do not crown the player King as an automatic quest reward.
Do not resolve the Senntisten mystery.
Do not make the palace a one-time dead endpoint.

## 17. Arrav's Planned Narrative Role

The world initially remembers Arrav as the monster who led the undead into Varrock.
Evidence gradually shows that he repeatedly resisted Zemouregal whenever control weakened.
During the Fall he regains enough control to create an evacuation opportunity.
He survives the Fall and later returns to the narrative.
His arc should culminate in public/personal redemption and direct confrontation with the truth of Zemouregal's control.
An older idea had Arrav fight beside the player in a final permanently victorious Battle of Varrock. The redemption remains; the permanent-reclamation ending does not.

## 18. Rank, Story, and the Crown

The player rises through Peasant → Commoner → Squire → Soldier → Knight → Lord → Minister → King, but the story does not hand out those ranks simply for completing chapters.
Quests establish eligibility and political trust.
War Effort, accomplishments, contribution, command, and service establish actual standing.
Knight is the first major social/military status and unlocks the companion system.
Lord is the first meaningful player-command tier.
Minister can start major Campaigns.
King can start the largest Conquests.
Veteran of Varrock should recognize meaningful contribution to a major assault and support later Minister progression.
King is a long-term endgame achievement, not automatically the final story reward.

## 19. Story / Gameplay Integration Rules

War Effort = personal lifetime service. Realm Supplies = shared consumable stockpile.
Normal Marches/Grand Marches are free scheduled public events. Higher player-commanded wars consume larger resources.
Rogue Knights are optional enemies/progression, not mandatory story/rank gates.
One active named companion maximum. Knight unlocks the system.
Classic OSRS bosses such as GWD and Barrows remain mechanically faithful and useful for real OSRS practice.
War-Forging extends high-end OSRS gear rather than replacing the original content that drops the base gear.
Normal RuneScape travel should generally remain usable. Regional stories restore/reveal special broken routes rather than making movement annoying.
Fallen Varrock can be explored outside wars. Major operations create deeper/higher-value opportunities without permanent territory state.

## 22. Pass 2 — Quest Design Work After Story Lock

Once the late story architecture is locked, each quest should receive a second-pass implementation brief containing only what development actually needs.
Quest purpose and story reveal.
Prerequisites that reflect genuine dependency.
NPCs and existing assets to reuse.
Required locations and whether any section needs an instance.
Existing systems reused (war, War Effort, Blast Furnace, Temple Trekking, transport, bosses, etc.).
New assets/mechanics only where they create a memorable payoff.
Major dialogue beats, not full scripts until structure is stable.
Rewards/unlocks and whether they are story unlocks, introductions, or optional progression.
Multiplayer/world-state compatibility check.
Development complexity / risk.
